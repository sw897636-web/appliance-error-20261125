# Candidates — The Funniest Honest Appliance Error

Scored against the house judge's five criteria (in order of importance),
each out of 5:

1. Honest error message an appliance could show (one line, ≤ 200 chars)
2. The laugh: a real joke / surprise / twist, not just cute phrasing
3. Honesty: names a plausible real fault; humour comes from the fault
4. Machine voice: flat, put-upon, sounds like the appliance itself
5. Economy: every word earns its place, no padding, no explaining the joke

## C1 — Washing machine, drain sock (WINNER, 122 chars)

> ERROR E24: Drain pump blocked by one sock. Its partner is in the dryer, dry and unbothered. I am drowning in your laundry.

| #1 | #2 | #3 | #4 | #5 | Total |
|----|----|----|----|----|-------|
| 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 4 | **24** |

Why it wins: a sock blocking a drain pump is a real, common washing-machine
fault (E24 is a real drain-fault code family). The joke is specific — one
sock lost, its partner safe in the dryer — and the last clause flips the
machine into the victim: it is the one drowning, in the owner's laundry.
Flat, blaming, deadpan throughout.

## Iteration log

- **Draft v1 (120 chars):** "…The other sock is in the dryer, dry and happy. I am drowning in your laundry."
- **Revision 1 → final (122 chars):** "The other sock … dry and happy" became "Its partner is in the dryer, dry and unbothered." "Partner" implies the pair without saying so; "unbothered" is colder and funnier than "happy" and keeps the deadpan register.
- **Revision 2 (considered, rejected):** shortening the last clause to "Meanwhile I am drowning." Rejected — "in your laundry" ties the punch back to the fault and blames the owner, earning its four extra words.
